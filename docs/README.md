

## Expected outcomes

By the end of the workshop, participants will have:

- A list of their campaigns' key claims, checked against the best available data, with the gaps named.
- A toolkit of free data sources and tools for climate and environmental issues.
- An additional information request per group.
- A campaign sketch per group that can be developed into a policy paper.

  - [Copernicus Interactive Climate Atlas](https://atlas.climate.copernicus.eu/): past and projected climate for any region

**Requesting information**

| Country | Law | Deadline |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Moldova | Law 148/2023 on access to information of public interest | 10 days, plus up to 7 if you are notified in writing within the first 10 |
| Romania | Law 544/2001 on free access to information of public interest | 10 days, up to 30 for complex requests if you are notified within the first 10 |
| Türkiye | Law 4982 on the right to information | 15 working days, up to 30 if another unit or institution is involved |

What to include:

- **Moldova:** your name and a **postal address**, even when you send it by email. A request without them can lawfully be ignored. You don't need to give a reason or sign it, and an electronic reply is free.
- **Romania:** your name and an address for the reply (email is fine), plus a signature if you send it on paper. You don't need to give a reason.
- **Türkiye:** your name, address and Turkish ID (T.C.) number. Requests can go through [CİMER](https://www.cimer.gov.tr/) or the institution's own form. Foreigners can only use the law if they live in Türkiye.
- **Everywhere:** ask for documents that already exist (monitoring data, permits, inspection reports, contracts, budgets), not for analysis or opinions, and ask for the reply in an electronic, editable format.

If you are refused or ignored:

- **Moldova:** go to the administrative court within 30 days of the reply, or within a year if there was no reply.
- **Romania:** complain to the head of the institution, or go to court, within 30 days. Court cases are free of court fees.
- **Türkiye:** object to the Board of Review of Access to Information (Bilgi Edinme Değerlendirme Kurulu) within 15 days.

Environmental information has extra protection. In Romania, Government Decision 878/2005 gives authorities one month (two for complex requests) and does not allow them to withhold data on emissions as a commercial secret. In Moldova, Law 148/2023 says the public interest outweighs the reasons for secrecy when information concerns harm to the environment.

Moldova and Romania are also parties to the UNECE Aarhus Convention on access to environmental information and public participation; Türkiye is not.

**Youth participation and climate**

- [Recommendation CM/Rec(2024)6 on young people and climate action](https://rm.coe.int/cm-rec-2024-6-young-people-and-climate-action/1680b21a0e) (Council of Europe Committee of Ministers, October 2024). It asks governments to encourage local and regional authorities to apply its measures.
- [European Charter on the Participation of Young People in Local and Regional Life](https://rm.coe.int/cg-2025-49-14finprov-en-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young/488028e792): a new Charter adopted by the Congress of Local and Regional Authorities in October 2025 and endorsed by the Committee of Ministers in March 2026. It names environment and climate change among the areas where young people's views must count, and covers online consultation and other forms of digital participation.
- Local youth bodies are consultative in all three countries:
  - Moldova: local youth councils (members aged 14–35) under a framework regulation approved by the Ministry of Education in 2025.
  - Romania: local youth councils that a local or county council may set up under the Youth Law (350/2006).
  - Türkiye: youth assemblies (gençlik meclisleri) within city councils (kent konseyleri), under Article 76 of the Municipal Law (5393).
- In Moldova, draft local decisions must be open for comments for at least 10 working days (Law 239/2008 on transparency in decision-making).

**Examples from the region**

- [Sofia Decides](https://www.interregeurope.eu/good-practices/sofia-decides-civic-participation-platform-for-transparent-local-decision-making) (Bulgaria): an open-source participation platform. More than 11,500 people voted in its first citizens' budget, which included a greening category.
- [Com'ON Cluj-Napoca](https://comoncluj.ro/) (Romania): a youth participatory budget run with the City Hall. Its Green Mission 2030 strand is tied to the city's climate neutrality goal.

## About the session

Young people don't need to wait for authorities to hand them information before taking part in climate decisions. A lot of the evidence already exists, and the right to ask for the rest is written into law in all three countries. Knowing where to look, how to check what you find and how to ask for what's missing turns participation from a request to be heard into a case that has to be answered.

This workshop invites participants to build that case for their own campaigns, together with peers from across the Black Sea Basin.

### About SG4Y-CLIMATE

[SG4Y-CLIMATE: Smart Governance for Youth Involvement in Climate Action](https://smartgovernance4youth.org/en) is an Interreg NEXT Black Sea Basin project co-financed by the European Union, running from November 2025 to May 2027. It is led by Istanbul Nişantaşı University, with ALDA Moldova, Galați Municipality and Tekirdağ Metropolitan Municipality as partners. TYCCA brings together young people from Moldova, Romania and Türkiye in four working groups.
