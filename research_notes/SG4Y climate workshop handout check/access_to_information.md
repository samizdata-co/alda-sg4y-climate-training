# Access to information and environmental information: Moldova, Romania, Türkiye (fact-check for the SG4Y climate workshop handout, as of November 2026)

Method note: the Moldovan Law 148/2023, Turkish Law 4982 and a consolidated Romanian Law 544/2001 (valid 10 May 2024 to 17 June 2026) were read in full text from PDF copies. legislatie.just.ro timed out and unece.org case pages returned 403, so some Romanian and Aarhus points rely on secondary copies. Each such point is flagged below.

## 1. Verification of the draft handout's claims

### Takeaway
All four country claims are broadly right, but several need sharper wording. Moldova's "10 + 7 days" is correct, but the law does not say "working days". Romania's 10/30 days is correct; the 2024 amendment (Law 122/2024) did not touch deadlines, and the "working days" rule comes from the implementing norms, not the law. Türkiye's 15/30 working days is correct. The Aarhus status is correct. The Moldova non-compliance case is **ACCC/C/2017/147**: communication filed July 2017 by Eco-TIRAS, findings adopted 25 July 2021, endorsed by the 7th Meeting of the Parties in October 2021.

### Cited Findings
**Moldova, Law 148/2023**
- The full title is Lege nr. 148 din 09.06.2023 privind accesul la informațiile de interes public. It was published in Monitorul Oficial no. 234, art. 410, on 08.07.2023 — [Law 148/2023 text, FAOLEX copy](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf)
- Art. 34 says it enters into force 6 months after publication and repeals Law 982/2000 on access to information — [Law 148/2023](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf). The entry-into-force date of 8 January 2024 is given by a secondary source — [Council of Europe IRIS Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/9800)
- Art. 19(1): information must be provided as soon as it is available, "but no later than 10 days from the date of registration of the request" ("nu mai târziu de 10 zile de la data înregistrării cererii") — [Law 148/2023](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf)
- Art. 19(2): the deadline can be extended by at most 7 days if the request is complex or covers a large volume of information. The extension only takes effect if the requester is told in writing, within the initial 10 days, with reasons — [Law 148/2023](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf). **The handout claim is correct.**
- Art. 19 says "zile" (days), whereas other articles of the same law say "zile lucrătoare" (working days): registration "next working day" (Art. 15), at least 5 working days to fix a defective request (Art. 17), 5 working days after payment (Art. 22(5)), 5 working days to forward a request (Art. 23(3)) — [Law 148/2023](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf)

**Romania, Law 544/2001**
- The consolidated text lists these amending acts: Law 371/2006, Law 380/2006, Law 188/2007, Law 76/2012, Law 144/2016 and **Law 122 of 30 April 2024** (MO 414 of 7 May 2024, applicable from 10 May 2024). The only Law 122/2024 change visible in that text is an added mention that the law transposes Art. 14(3) of Directive (EU) 2016/800 (child suspects in criminal proceedings). Deadlines were not changed — [Law 544/2001 consolidated, valid 10.05.2024 to 17.06.2026 (ISPAIM copy)](https://ispaim.mapn.ro/webroot/fileslib/upload/files/Legislatie/Legea%20544%20din%202001%20privind%20liberul%20acces%20la%20informatiile%20de%20interes%20public.pdf)
- Art. 7(1): the authority must answer in writing "within 10 days or, as the case may be, at most 30 days from registration". If identifying the information takes more than 10 days, the 30-day limit applies only if the requester is told in writing within 10 days. Art. 7(2): a refusal must be reasoned and communicated within 5 days. Art. 7(3): information may be requested and obtained electronically "if the technical conditions are met" — [Law 544/2001 consolidated](https://ispaim.mapn.ro/webroot/fileslib/upload/files/Legislatie/Legea%20544%20din%202001%20privind%20liberul%20acces%20la%20informatiile%20de%20interes%20public.pdf)
- A proposal to amend Law 544 (B591/2025) received a Senate opinion of 18 November 2025 recommending rejection — [Senat opinion B591/2025](https://www.senat.ro/PAGINI/OPINII/2025/11/18/96da89e9-4031-4c1c-bc29-fc35850298fb/Opinie_B591_2025.pdf). The outcome was not confirmed.
- A 2021 parliamentary bill would have required electronic, open, machine-readable answers, a public online register of requests and fines of 500–7,500 lei. In March 2021 it was still at committee/plenary stage — [G4Media, 22 March 2021](https://www.g4media.ro/acces-mai-usor-la-informatiile-de-interes-public-toate-institutiile-finantate-din-bani-publici-obligate-sa-ofere-informatii-fiecare-institutie-publica-obligata-sa-aiba-un-registru-propriu-cu-solic.html). Its provisions do not appear in the consolidated law text up to June 2026, so it seems it was not enacted in that form.
- The relevant 2022 change is to the **methodological norms**, not the law: a municipal citizen's guide says the current norms are **HG 830/2022** — [Primăria Videle, Ghidul cetățeanului Legea 544](https://primariavidele.ro/attachments/article/557/Ghidul-Cetateanului-Legea-544_SGD.pdf)

**Türkiye, Law 4982**
- Bilgi Edinme Hakkı Kanunu, Law no. 4982, adopted 9/10/2003, Resmî Gazete 24/10/2003 no. 25269 — [mevzuat.gov.tr, Law 4982](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.5.4982.pdf)
- Art. 11: access must be given within **15 working days** ("onbeş iş günü"). It is **30 working days** if the information comes from another unit of the same institution, if another institution's opinion is needed, or if the request concerns more than one institution. The extension and its reason must be notified in writing before the 15 working days run out — [Law 4982](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.5.4982.pdf). **The handout claim is correct**, with this nuance: the extension covers internal units as well as other institutions, and it must be notified.

**Aarhus Convention status**
- UN Treaty Collection (XXVII-13): Republic of Moldova signed 25 Jun 1998 and ratified 9 Aug 1999. Romania signed 25 Jun 1998 and ratified 11 Jul 2000. Türkiye is not listed (neither signatory nor party). There are 48 parties — [UN Treaty Collection, Aarhus Convention status](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XXVII-13&chapter=27&clang=_en). **The handout claim is correct.**

## 2. Deadlines and extensions: calendar or working days?

### Takeaway
- **Moldova:** 10 days, plus up to 7; the type of day is not stated.
- **Romania:** the law says 10 days, or up to 30 for complex requests, and 5 days for refusals, without specifying the type of day. The methodological norms say working days. Sources conflict, and one municipal guide says calendar days.
- **Türkiye:** 15 working days, or 30 working days, both explicit.
- For environmental information, Romania's HG 878/2005 gives a different, longer timeline: 1 month, or 2 months.

### Cited Findings
- **Moldova:**
  - Art. 19 sets 10 days, plus up to 7, as above.
  - Linked requests run from the first request's registration date (Art. 19(3)).
  - Oral requests are answered the same day if the information is available. Otherwise the requester is told to file in writing or electronically (Art. 19(4)).
  - If the authority forwards a request to another holder (within 5 working days), a new 10-day term starts (Art. 23(3)–(4)).
  - Not answering within the legal term counts as a rejection (Art. 23(5)).
  - Source: [Law 148/2023](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf)
- **Romania:**
  - Art. 7(1)–(2) of the law say "10 zile", "30 de zile" and "5 zile", with no "lucrătoare" — [Law 544/2001 consolidated](https://ispaim.mapn.ro/webroot/fileslib/upload/files/Legislatie/Legea%20544%20din%202001%20privind%20liberul%20acces%20la%20informatiile%20de%20interes%20public.pdf)
  - The methodological norms (Art. 16) set 10 working days to provide information, 10 working days to notify an extension, 30 working days for information identified later, and 5 working days for a refusal — [Lege5, Norma metodologică Legea 544](https://lege5.ro/gratuit/gqydknby/norma-metodologica-de-aplicare-a-legii-nr-544-2001-privind-liberul-acces-la-informatiile-de-interes-public-din-07022002). The page says it shows the text as published in 2002 "without later amendments", so the current wording under HG 830/2022 is not confirmed.
  - An institution's March 2026 information page also says 10 / 30 / 5 working days — [ISU Iași, Informare Legea 544](https://isuis.igsu.ro/aplicarea-legii-5442001/informare-legea-5442001-21)
  - **This is contradicted** by a municipal citizen's guide based on HG 830/2022, which says "maximum 10 calendar days" — [Primăria Videle guide](https://primariavidele.ro/attachments/article/557/Ghidul-Cetateanului-Legea-544_SGD.pdf)
- **Romania, environmental information (HG 878/2005):**
  - Art. 4(1): information must be provided as soon as possible, taking into account any deadline the requester names, and at the latest within **one month** of receipt.
  - Art. 4(2): **two months** if volume and complexity make one month impossible. The requester must be told, with reasons, before the one month expires.
  - A request forwarded to the right authority must go within 15 days (Art. 11(1)(a)).
  - Source: [HG 878/2005 (Ministry of Environment copy, consolidated to 2009)](https://www.mmediu.ro/app/webroot/uploads/files/HG_878_2005.pdf)
  - The act was later amended, including by HG 304/2024 — [search snippet citing consolidated HG 878/2005](https://www.cjsm.ro/storage/2023/hotararea-nr-878-din-2005-privind-accesul-publicului-la-informatia-privind-mediul.pdf). The content of the 2024 change was not verified.
- **Türkiye:**
  - Art. 11: 15 working days, or 30 working days.
  - If the authority notifies a fee, the 15-working-day clock stops. If the requester does not pay within 15 working days, the request is treated as withdrawn.
  - Source: [Law 4982](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.5.4982.pdf)
- **Moldova, environmental information:**
  - Law 148/2023 Art. 2(3): "Access to environmental information is carried out in the manner established by the Government" — [Law 148/2023](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf)
  - The existing regulation is HG 1467/2016, in force July 2017 — [ENI SEIS newsletter, 24.03.2017](https://eni-seis.eionet.europa.eu/east/areas-of-work/communication/newsletter/24-03-2017-moldova-governmental-decision-on-access-to-environmental-information-from-dec-2016-that-will-enter-into-force-in-july-2017)
  - A gov.md file presented as that regulation gives **15 working days**, or **20 working days** for voluminous or complex requests — [gov.md attachment intr28_25](https://gov.md/sites/default/files/document/attachments/intr28_25.pdf). Seen only via a search snippet; it may be a draft.
  - A 2024 draft Government decision would approve a new regulation on access to environmental information, participation and justice, and repeal HG 1467/2016 — [particip.gov.md draft](https://particip.gov.md/ro/download_attachment/25273)

### Inferences
- Practical advice for Romania: tell participants to expect an answer within 10 days, but not to treat a reply on day 11–14 (calendar) as late until 10 working days have passed. The norms and most institutions use working days, and this keeps a complaint from being dismissed as early.
- For environmental requests in Romania, it is worth citing both Law 544/2001 and HG 878/2005. The authority must apply the 10-day rule of Law 544, while HG 878 adds stronger environmental protections (emissions rule, public-interest test). Whether the shorter general deadline prevails over the one-month environmental deadline is not settled in the sources found.
- For Moldova, a request about the environment may fall under the special Government regulation, which may allow a longer deadline (reportedly 15/20 working days). Citing Law 148/2023 is still advisable.

### Gaps
- The current text of the Romanian norms (HG 123/2002 as amended by HG 830/2022) was not read from an official source.
- Whether the 2024 Moldovan draft environmental-information regulation was adopted, and the current official deadlines in HG 1467/2016, could not be verified on legis.md.
- The changes HG 304/2024 made to Romania's HG 878/2005 were not verified.

## 3. Email or online requests, ID, reasons, fees

### Takeaway
- **Moldova:** email is explicitly allowed, no signature or e-signature is needed, no reason is required, and electronic delivery is free. A name and postal address are mandatory.
- **Romania:** email is allowed. Name and an address for the reply are required; the law also mentions a signature. No reason is needed, and it is free apart from copy costs.
- **Türkiye:** a petition needs name, signature and address. Electronic requests (CİMER, e-Devlet or an institution's form/email) require identity verification, in practice the T.C. kimlik number. Foreigners have only limited rights. No reason is required, and cost-based fees are possible.

### Cited Findings
**Moldova**
- Art. 14: a request can be electronic, written or posted, or oral (in person or by phone). Art. 14(2): electronic requests do not have to meet the legal requirements for electronic documents.
- Art. 15(2): the authority must email back the registration number and date by the next working day.
- Art. 16(1) mandatory elements: (a) name and surname; (b) **postal address**, plus email if an emailed reply is wanted; (c) the name of the authority; (d) a clear specification of the information.
- Art. 16(3): no reason or justification is required. Art. 16(4): a handwritten or electronic signature is not mandatory.
- Art. 17(1): a request without the name or postal address **is not examined**. Art. 17(2): if the information requested is unclear, the authority must say so, help, and give at least 5 working days to fix it.
- Art. 22: free if provided electronically, by post up to 20 pages, on site up to 20 pages, by inspecting originals, or orally. Above 20 pages the charge is 0.02 conventional units (1 CU = 50 lei, so 1 leu per page). Charges cannot exceed actual costs.
- Art. 6(1): any natural or legal person may request information.
- Source: [Law 148/2023](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf)

**Romania**
- Art. 6(1): "any person" may request. Art. 6(3): a written request must contain the authority, the information requested, and "the name, surname and signature of the requester, as well as the address to which the reply should be sent" — [Law 544/2001 consolidated](https://ispaim.mapn.ro/webroot/fileslib/upload/files/Legislatie/Legea%20544%20din%202001%20privind%20liberul%20acces%20la%20informatiile%20de%20interes%20public.pdf)
- Methodological norms: Art. 15(2) allows requests and administrative complaints by email using the model forms (Annex 1, 2a, 2b). Art. 20(4): the requester must get written confirmation of the registration date and number. In Annex 1, email is optional and profession and phone are optional — [Lege5, Norma metodologică](https://lege5.ro/gratuit/gqydknby/norma-metodologica-de-aplicare-a-legii-nr-544-2001-privind-liberul-acces-la-informatiile-de-interes-public-din-07022002). This is the 2002 text.
- The municipal guide based on HG 830/2022 says:
  - an email address is enough as the reply address, and a home address is no longer required if a reply by email is wanted;
  - institutions must accept electronic forms;
  - no reason is needed;
  - information is free, with charges only for physical copies of bulky documents.
  - Source: [Primăria Videle guide](https://primariavidele.ro/attachments/article/557/Ghidul-Cetateanului-Legea-544_SGD.pdf). This is secondary and was not confirmed in the HG 830/2022 text.
- HG 878/2005 Art. 3(3): environmental information must be provided to any requester "without justification of the purpose". Art. 15(2): a refusal is sent in writing or electronically if the request was written or the requester asked for that. Art. 29: access to public registers and on-site examination are free. Arts. 30–31 (fees) are repealed in the 2009 consolidated text — [HG 878/2005](https://www.mmediu.ro/app/webroot/uploads/files/HG_878_2005.pdf)

**Türkiye**
- Art. 4: "everyone" has the right. Foreigners resident in Türkiye and foreign legal entities active there may use it only for information about themselves or their field of activity, and on a reciprocity basis.
- Art. 6: the petition must include name, surname, signature, and residence or work address. A request may be made electronically or by other means if the person's identity and signature, or the origin of the text, can be legally established. The information requested must be stated clearly.
- Art. 10: the institution may charge a fee equal to the cost of access. Art. 12: answers are given in writing or electronically, and a refusal must state its reasons and the appeal routes.
- Source: [Law 4982](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.5.4982.pdf)
- No reason is required: Law 4982 contains no such requirement (based on reading the full text above).
- In practice, email or fax requests must include the **T.C. identity number** and the regulation's EK-1 form (individuals) or EK-2 form (legal entities). Requests missing these are not processed (citing the Implementing Regulation, Art. 14) — [Presidency Strategy and Budget Directorate, Bilgi Edinme page](https://www.sbb.gov.tr/bilgi-edinme/)
- The Ministry of Foreign Affairs says online information requests are handled via **CİMER**, and that name, surname, T.C. number, address and email are mandatory for a reply — [MFA, Bilgi Edinme ve Diğer Başvurular](https://www.mfa.gov.tr/bilgi-edinme.tr.mfa)

### Inferences
- Moldova: participants must include a postal address even when sending by email. Otherwise the request can lawfully be ignored (Art. 17(1)). This is the single most important "first request" tip for Moldova.
- Türkiye: foreign participants without Turkish residence probably cannot use Law 4982 in their own name. A Turkish citizen or a Türkiye-registered NGO should file. Requests need the T.C. kimlik no, so they cannot be anonymous.
- Romania: to be safe, include name, an email address for the reply, and (if on paper) a signature. Email requests are standard practice.

### Gaps
- The text of the Turkish Implementing Regulation (Bilgi Edinme Hakkı Kanununun Uygulanmasına İlişkin Esas ve Usuller Hakkında Yönetmelik) was not read directly.
- Whether Romania's HG 830/2022 formally removed the signature requirement for email requests was not confirmed. The law itself still says "signature".

## 4. Environmental-information regimes (special rules)

### Takeaway
- **Romania** has a full EU-style regime (HG 878/2005, transposing Directive 2003/4/EC). It has strong pro-disclosure rules: a public-interest test, and emissions information cannot be withheld on most grounds.
- **Moldova** delegates environmental information to a Government regulation (HG 1467/2016, possibly being replaced).
- **Türkiye** has no separate regime. The Environment Law defines "environmental information" broadly, but requests go through Law 4982.

### Cited Findings
- **Romania, HG 878/2005:**
  - Art. 11(1): refusal grounds — the information is not held (then it must be forwarded within 15 days), the request is manifestly unreasonable or too general, the material is unfinished, or internal communications.
  - Art. 11(2): if refused as unfinished, the authority must name who is preparing the material and the estimated completion date.
  - Art. 12(1): refusal grounds where disclosure would affect confidentiality of proceedings, international relations, security or defence, the course of justice, commercial or industrial confidentiality, intellectual property, personal data, a voluntary supplier's interests, or protection of the environment (e.g. location of rare species).
  - Art. 12(2)–(3): all grounds must be interpreted **restrictively**, weighing the public interest in disclosure case by case.
  - Art. 12(4): requests about **emissions into the environment** cannot be refused on grounds (a), (d), (f), (g) or (h) (confidentiality of proceedings, commercial confidentiality, personal data, voluntary supplier, environmental protection).
  - Art. 15(1): partial disclosure where separable.
  - Source: [HG 878/2005](https://www.mmediu.ro/app/webroot/uploads/files/HG_878_2005.pdf)
  - A 2024 Ministry of Environment order (Ordinul 1608/2024) approved a guide for public authorities on access to environmental information, published by many councils in July–September 2024 — [CJ Teleorman, Ghidul autorităților publice… 18-07-2024](https://cjteleorman.ro/agenda/Ghidul%20autoritatilor%20publice%20pentru%20accesul%20publicului%20la%20informatia%20de%20mediu%20din%2018-07-2024.pdf); [Ordin 1608/2024 copy](https://luncamuresului.ro/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Ordin_1608_2024.pdf). Seen as search titles only; contents not read.
- **Moldova:**
  - Law 148/2023 Art. 2(3) delegates environmental information to the Government. Art. 9(3)(f) says the public interest prevails in particular for information whose disclosure would help prevent or reveal **harm to the environment**. Art. 9(3)(e) does the same for serious threats to life or health — [Law 148/2023](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf)
  - HG 1467/2016 is the environmental-information regulation, in force July 2017 — [ENI SEIS](https://eni-seis.eionet.europa.eu/east/areas-of-work/communication/newsletter/24-03-2017-moldova-governmental-decision-on-access-to-environmental-information-from-dec-2016-that-will-enter-into-force-in-july-2017)
  - A 2024 draft replacement exists — [particip.gov.md](https://particip.gov.md/ro/download_attachment/25273)
- **Türkiye:**
  - Environment Law no. 2872, Art. 2 (amended 2006 by Law 5491) defines "çevreye ilişkin bilgi" as all written, oral or visual information or data on water, air, soil, flora and fauna, on activities that harm or may harm them, and on administrative and technical measures taken — [Çevre Kanunu text, alomaliye.com](https://www.alomaliye.com/2000/08/11/cevre-kanunu/amp/). This is secondary; the official text is on mevzuat.gov.tr.
  - Secondary sources say Art. 30 ("Bilgi edinme ve başvuru") refers to Law 4982 rather than setting its own procedure — [Lexpera literature](https://www.lexpera.com.tr/literatur/kitaplar/3-4-2-katilim-ilkesinin-mevzuattaki-yeri-978-625-432-941-8). Not verified against the primary text.

### Inferences
- For Romanian climate/pollution requests (air quality, emissions from a plant, landfill data), cite HG 878/2005 Art. 12(4). Emissions data cannot be withheld as "commercial secret" or "personal data".
- For Moldova, quote Law 148/2023 Art. 9(3)(f) (public interest prevails for preventing environmental harm) when an authority invokes an exemption.
- For Türkiye, the same Law 4982 deadlines and exemptions apply. There are no extra environmental safeguards.

### Gaps
- The primary text of Art. 30 of Turkish Law 2872 was not retrieved.
- The current official text of Moldova's HG 1467/2016, and the adoption status of its replacement, were not retrieved.

## 5. Common exemptions and how to appeal

### Takeaway
- **Moldova:** go straight to administrative court. No prior complaint is needed. You have 30 days after a reply, or 1 year if there is no reply. Courts can fine the authority. The People's Advocate monitors compliance.
- **Romania:** either an administrative complaint to the head of the institution (30 days), or a court case directly (30 days after the deadline expires). Court cases are free and urgent.
- **Türkiye:** an optional objection to the Bilgi Edinme Değerlendirme Kurulu within 15 days. The Board decides within 30 working days, and the objection pauses the court deadline. After that, administrative court.

### Cited Findings
**Moldova**
- Art. 8(1): access may be limited only if disclosure would harm public safety, international relations, prevention or detection of offences, criminal prosecution, administrative or judicial proceedings, personal data, intellectual property or commercial secrets. Art. 8(2): also where a law expressly restricts access (state, banking or medical secret).
- Art. 8(3): officials' professional data (name, position, education, experience, pay, conflicts of interest, disciplinary sanctions) cannot be withheld as personal data.
- Art. 9: a cumulative harm test plus a public-interest override.
- Art. 24: refusals must be in writing with the legal ground and the appeal route. Partial disclosure is mandatory. Information cannot be refused because it is spread across documents or "not systematised".
- Art. 25: challenge in administrative court ("contencios administrativ") **without a prior procedure**, within 30 days of the reply. If there is no reply in time, within **one year** of the deadline expiring.
- Art. 26: the court decides in written procedure within 3 months (extendable by 3).
- Arts. 28–29: courts fine the information provider 5–60 CU (1 CU = 50 lei) for not publishing, 10–100 CU for incomplete answers, illegal fees or unfounded rejection or forwarding, and 20–200 CU for unfounded refusal.
- Arts. 30–31: the **People's Advocate (Ombudsman)** monitors compliance under Law 52/2014. The State Chancellery publishes an annual report by end of March.
- Source: [Law 148/2023](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf)
- The State Chancellery's 2024 report on how requests were handled — [gov.md report 2024](https://gov.md/sites/default/files/users-media/media-15/Raport%20privind%20solu%C8%9Bionarea%20cererilor%20de%20comunicare%20a%20informa%C8%9Biilor%20de%20interes%20public%20%C3%AEn%20anul%202024.pdf). **Romania**
- Art. 12(1) exemptions:
  - (a) classified defence or public-order information;
  - (b) classified deliberations or economic and political interests;
  - (c) commercial or financial activities, if disclosure harms intellectual property or fair competition;
  - (d) personal data;
  - (e) criminal or disciplinary investigations, if disclosure endangers them;
  - (f) judicial proceedings, if disclosure harms a fair trial;
  - (g) information whose publication harms the protection of young people.
- Art. 21(2): an administrative complaint ("reclamație") to the head of the institution within 30 days of learning of the refusal. Art. 21(3): if it is upheld, the reply (with the information) is due within 15 days.
- Art. 22: complaint to the administrative-litigation section of the tribunal (where the requester lives or where the authority sits), within 30 days of the Art. 7 deadline expiring. The court can order disclosure and moral or material damages. Cases are judged urgently and are exempt from stamp duty (Art. 22(5)).
- Source: [Law 544/2001 consolidated](https://ispaim.mapn.ro/webroot/fileslib/upload/files/Legislatie/Legea%20544%20din%202001%20privind%20liberul%20acces%20la%20informatiile%20de%20interes%20public.pdf)
- You can go straight to court without the administrative complaint — [Primăria Videle guide](https://primariavidele.ro/attachments/article/557/Ghidul-Cetateanului-Legea-544_SGD.pdf). This is consistent with Art. 22 being independent of Art. 21.
- Environmental information: a prior complaint ("plângere prealabilă") to the head of the authority under Art. 7 of Law 554/2004, free of charge, then the administrative court (HG 878/2005 Arts. 16–17) — [HG 878/2005](https://www.mmediu.ro/app/webroot/uploads/files/HG_878_2005.pdf)

**Türkiye**
- Exemptions:
  - Art. 15: judicial-review-exempt acts (with an exception for acts affecting working life).
  - Art. 16: classified state secrets.
  - Art. 17: the country's economic interests.
  - Art. 18: intelligence.
  - Art. 19: administrative investigations.
  - Art. 20: judicial investigations and prosecutions.
  - Art. 21: privacy, including health data. Personal data may be disclosed in the public interest with the person's written consent after at least 7 days' notice.
  - Art. 22: confidentiality of communications.
  - Art. 23: trade secrets.
  - Art. 24: intellectual and artistic works.
  - Art. 25: internal regulations.
  - Art. 26: internal opinions and recommendations are **in** scope unless decided otherwise.
  - Art. 27: requests for advice or opinions are excluded.
- Other refusal grounds:
  - Art. 7: requests that would require a separate study, research, examination or analysis can be refused. Information held elsewhere is forwarded.
  - Art. 8: already-published information is not subject to requests, but the authority must say where and when it was published.
  - Art. 9: classified parts must be separated out and the rest disclosed, with reasons.
- Art. 13: a requester whose request is refused may object to the **Bilgi Edinme Değerlendirme Kurulu** within **15 days** of notification, before going to court. The Board decides within **30 working days**. An objection suspends the time limit for going to administrative court.
- Art. 14: the Board has 9 members appointed by the President. Its secretariat is the Ministry of Justice (after KHK 703 of 2018).
- Art. 29: disciplinary sanctions for officials. Information obtained cannot be reproduced or used for commercial purposes.
- Source: [Law 4982](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuatmetin/1.5.4982.pdf)
- BEDK publishes its decisions and principle decisions ("ilke kararları") online — [BEDK, 2021/1 principle decision](https://bedk.adalet.gov.tr/Resimler/SayfaDokuman/2510202108552105.05.2021%20TAR%C4%B0H%20VE%202021-1%20SAYILI%20%C4%B0LKE%20KARARI.pdf)

### Inferences
- The exemptions authorities most often cite against environmental requests are likely to be commercial secrets (Romania 12(1)(c), Moldova 8(1)(h), Türkiye Art. 23), personal data, and "information not held / requires analysis" (Türkiye Art. 7). Coaching participants to ask for **existing documents** (permits, inspection reports, monitoring data, contracts) rather than analyses or opinions avoids the Türkiye Art. 7 and Art. 27 traps.
- In Moldova there is no internal-appeal step. The handout should not tell Moldovan participants to "complain to the People's Advocate first". The Ombudsman is a monitoring body and an optional route, while the court is the statutory remedy.

### Gaps
- Türkiye: the time limit for administrative court is usually 60 days under İYUK (Law 2577, Art. 7), and implied refusal applies after 30 days of silence (İYUK Art. 10). Neither was verified from a primary source here. Law 4982 itself does not set a deemed-refusal period.
- Whether Türkiye's Ombudsman (Kamu Denetçiliği Kurumu) accepts complaints about information refusals was not researched.
- Whether Moldova's People's Advocate takes individual complaints specifically on Law 148/2023 was not researched beyond the law's reference to Law 52/2014.

## 6. Official portals and practical NGO tips

### Takeaway
- **Türkiye:** use CİMER (cimer.gov.tr, also via e-Devlet) or each institution's EK-1 form or information email.
- **Romania:** reportedly has a central request platform on e-consultare.gov.ro since 2026, in addition to institutions' Law 544 email addresses (this needs checking).
- **Moldova:** no central portal was found. Requests go by email to each authority.
- NGO-specific guides from Funky Citizens, ActiveWatch, IDIS, API or Turkish NGOs were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Türkiye: online information requests are handled through the CİMER system — [MFA](https://www.mfa.gov.tr/bilgi-edinme.tr.mfa). Institutions also publish an information email and the EK-1/EK-2 forms, e.g. bilgi@sbb.gov.tr — [SBB Bilgi Edinme](https://www.sbb.gov.tr/bilgi-edinme/)
- Romania: according to the municipal guide, e-consultare.gov.ro (run by the General Secretariat of the Government) has been integrated for Law 544 requests "officially since 2026". You select county and institution, the request is routed to the responsible official, and replies, extension notices and refusals arrive electronically. Statistics appear on info-transparenta.gov.ro — [Primăria Videle guide](https://primariavidele.ro/attachments/article/557/Ghidul-Cetateanului-Legea-544_SGD.pdf). **Secondary and unverified.**
- Romania: each institution must publish a responsible person, contact details and model forms. The usual email patterns are relatiipublice@… or 544@…. Check whether the information is already published proactively ("comunicare din oficiu") before asking, and use written or email requests for traceability — [Primăria Videle guide](https://primariavidele.ro/attachments/article/557/Ghidul-Cetateanului-Legea-544_SGD.pdf)
- Romania: a model request form (Annex 1 of the norms) asks the requester to list the documents "as concretely as possible" — [ANM model form](https://www.anm.ro/_/INFORMATII%20PUBLICE/Formular-tip%20cerere%20de%20informa%C5%A3ii%20de%20interes%20public.pdf); [Lege5 norms](https://lege5.ro/gratuit/gqydknby/norma-metodologica-de-aplicare-a-legii-nr-544-2001-privind-liberul-acces-la-informatiile-de-interes-public-din-07022002)
- Moldova: the authority must register a request and email back the registration number by the next working day (Art. 15(2)). Authorities must help requesters specify the information (Art. 17(2)). Several requests can be merged into one answer (Art. 18) — [Law 148/2023](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/mol221241.pdf)
- Moldova NGO commentary: the Legal Resources Centre (CRJM) published opinions on the draft law in 2023 — [CRJM opinion, March 2023](https://crjm.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2023-03-Opinie-accesul-la-informatii-publice-CRJM.pdf); [CRJM opinion, May 2023](https://crjm.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/2023-05-Opinie-acces-la-informatii-CRJM-1.pdf). Not read.

### Inferences
Practical first-request checklist, drawn from the legal texts above:
1. Check the authority's website first. In Türkiye, already-published information is refused with a pointer (Art. 8 of Law 4982).
2. Ask for specific existing documents or datasets with dates and locations, e.g. "the environmental permit for X", "air-quality monitoring data for station Y, January–June 2026", "inspection reports on landfill Z since 2024". Do not ask for analyses or opinions.
3. Cite the law. In Romania, cite HG 878/2005 for environmental data; in Moldova, Art. 9(3)(f) of Law 148/2023.
4. Give the identity data the law requires:
   - Moldova: name and **postal address**, plus email.
   - Romania: name and a reply address (email).
   - Türkiye: name, address, signature or T.C. no.
5. Ask for an electronic reply in an open format.
6. Keep the registration number and diary the deadline.
7. Know the appeal clock:
   - Moldova: 30 days or 1 year to go to court.
   - Romania: 30 days, by complaint or court.
   - Türkiye: 15 days to the Board.
