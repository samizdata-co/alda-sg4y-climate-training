# Youth participation frameworks (climate and local decision-making): Moldova, Romania, Türkiye and European level. Verification notes, as of 3 November 2026

Method note: Primary texts were read in full where I could get them: the CM/Rec(2024)6 PDF, the Congress report CG(2025)49-14 with Resolution 516 (2025) and the Charter text, Moldova's OMEC 2057/2025 as published, the Tekirdağ TYCCA call, and the Eurobarometer FL013EP Romania factsheet. Other points rest on search snippets or secondary pages and are flagged. legis.md, legislatie.just.ro and public.ps2.ro could not be fetched (403 errors or refused connections), so the national transparency-law deadlines were not checked against consolidated texts.

## 1. Claims to verify: CM/Rec(2024)6 and the European Charter on youth participation in local and regional life

### Takeaway
CM/Rec(2024)6 checks out: adopted 23 October 2024 at the 1510th meeting of the Ministers' Deputies, with an explicit clause inviting local and regional authorities to apply it. The "first international instrument" label comes from ERYICA (secondary), not from the text itself, so the handout should attribute it or soften it. The Charter claim also checks out: the Congress adopted the new Charter on 29 October 2025 through Resolution 516 (2025), together with Recommendation 537 (2025). The Committee of Ministers then adopted it as CM/Rec(2026)3 on 12 March 2026. The handout should mention this, because CM/Rec(2026)3 replaces Rec(2004)13.

### Cited Findings
**CM/Rec(2024)6 (young people and climate action)**
- Title and date: "Recommendation CM/Rec(2024)6 of the Committee of Ministers to member States on young people and climate action (Adopted by the Committee of Ministers on 23 October 2024 at the 1510th meeting of the Ministers' Deputies)". — [CM/Rec(2024)6 text, rm.coe.int](https://rm.coe.int/cm-rec-2024-6-young-people-and-climate-action/1680b21a0e)
- The operative paragraphs ask governments to: (1) apply the appendix measures, which "protect the rights of young people and young environmental defenders; remove barriers to their participation in climate-related decision-making processes" and ensure "full access to climate-related information and data"; (3) translate and disseminate it "especially among national, regional and local authorities"; (4) "invite local and regional authorities to implement those measures in this recommendation that fall within their sphere of competence"; (5) review implementation five years after adoption (so around October 2029) and every five years after that. — [CM/Rec(2024)6 text](https://rm.coe.int/cm-rec-2024-6-young-people-and-climate-action/1680b21a0e)
- Appendix aims include "ensure meaningful participation in climate-related decision-making processes by reaching out to and engaging in dialogue with young people and by establishing and strengthening accessible consultation mechanisms and platforms". — [CM/Rec(2024)6 text](https://rm.coe.int/cm-rec-2024-6-young-people-and-climate-action/1680b21a0e)
- The "Strengthening youth participation" measures are the ones most relevant to local authorities. They are: consult young people on climate-crisis needs; "diverse representation and meaningful participation ... in climate policy decision-making processes at all levels", integrated "into all relevant sectors", not only youth policy; promote "regulatory and legislative processes that actively involve young people ... at the local, regional and national levels"; provide capacity building and financial support so youth organisations can "co-create and evaluate actions with the authorities"; and respect the autonomy of youth-led climate movements. — [CM/Rec(2024)6 text](https://rm.coe.int/cm-rec-2024-6-young-people-and-climate-action/1680b21a0e)
- Other measure clusters: access to rights (freedom of assembly and expression, protection of young environmental defenders, judicial review in environmental matters, considering national recognition of the right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment); green jobs, education and green skills (micro-funding for young green entrepreneurs, dedicated funding for youth organisations' climate projects); support for youth workers; youth-friendly health services, including for mental well-being affected by climate change; and climate information in accessible formats and minority languages. — [CM/Rec(2024)6 text](https://rm.coe.int/cm-rec-2024-6-young-people-and-climate-action/1680b21a0e)
- The text cites the Aarhus Convention and Rec(2004)13 on youth participation in local and regional life. — [CM/Rec(2024)6 text](https://rm.coe.int/cm-rec-2024-6-young-people-and-climate-action/1680b21a0e)
- "First international instrument": ERYICA calls it the first international instrument explicitly addressing young people and climate action. The recommendation text does not say this. — [ERYICA news](https://eryica.org/news/coe-climate-change-recommendations); CoE announcement: [coe.int youth news](https://www.coe.int/en/web/youth/-/adopted-new-recommendation-on-young-people-and-climate-action) (not opened in full)

**European Charter on the Participation of Young People in Local and Regional Life (2025)**
- Report CG(2025)49-14, 29 October 2025, 49th session, Committee on Social Inclusion and Human Dignity; rapporteurs Aida Karimli (Sweden) and Roberto Pella (Italy). It contains Resolution 516 (2025), Recommendation 537 (2025) and the Charter. Footnote: "Debated and adopted by the Congress on 29 October 2025". — [CG(2025)49-14, rm.coe.int](https://rm.coe.int/cg-2025-49-14finprov-en-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young/488028e792)
- It explicitly replaces the 2003 text. Resolution 516 refers to Recommendation 128 (2003) and Rec(2004)13 and to "recent evolutions ... which justify that the texts be replaced by the present ones". It builds on the original 1992 Charter and the revised 2003 Charter, and the revision began in 2024 together with the Joint Council on Youth. CM/Rec(2024)6 appears in the list of instruments it draws on. — [CG(2025)49-14](https://rm.coe.int/cg-2025-49-14finprov-en-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young/488028e792)
- Committee of Ministers follow-up: the Ministers' Deputies adopted the Charter as a recommendation to member States at their 1554th meeting on 12 March 2026. A later CM reply to PACE numbers it CM/Rec(2026)3. — [CM meeting 12 March 2026](https://www.coe.int/en/web/cm/-/meeting-of-the-ministers-deputies-on-12-march-2026); [CoE youth news "Adopted!"](https://www.coe.int/web/youth/-/adopted-the-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young-people-in-local-and-regional-life); [CoE portal news](https://www.coe.int/en/web/portal/-/youth-democratic-participation-especially-at-local-level-essential-in-times-of-stability-and-crisis-alike). I saw the number only in a search snippet and did not open the PACE document.

**What changed compared with 2003 (taken from the 2025 Charter text and explanatory memorandum)**
- New framing: a human-rights-based approach in which local and regional authorities are "duty-bearers of youth participation"; participation means "the right, the means, the space and the structures, the opportunities and the tools, as well as the full support". The 2003 formula was "right, means, space, opportunity and support". — [CG(2025)49-14](https://rm.coe.int/cg-2025-49-14finprov-en-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young/488028e792)
- New structure: Chapters on principles, spaces and structures, youth policy and youth perspectives (Ch. IV), opportunities and tools (Ch. V), and a new section on dissemination, evaluation and review. The memorandum notes that "the 2003 Charter did not include provisions in this respect and no mechanism to review the implementation ... was set up". — [CG(2025)49-14](https://rm.coe.int/cg-2025-49-14finprov-en-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young/488028e792)
- Environment and climate: para. 48 requires that youth perspectives be taken into account in policy areas including "environment and climate change" and "digital technologies and digitalisation, social media and artificial intelligence". The summary says youth participation in "political, social, environmental and economic life" is needed for sustainable development. Para. 49 covers keeping youth participation going during "crisis, conflict, displacement, or natural disasters, including alternative, cross-border or digital means of participation". — [CG(2025)49-14](https://rm.coe.int/cg-2025-49-14finprov-en-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young/488028e792)
- Digital participation: para. 40 says authorities "should consider leveraging inclusive, accessible and safe digital platforms ... including online consultations, virtual town halls and interactive decision-making platforms", including in emergencies. Para. 41 covers digital youth work and mobile youth centres for rural and remote youth. Media literacy and the digital divide come up in the consultation findings. — [CG(2025)49-14](https://rm.coe.int/cg-2025-49-14finprov-en-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young/488028e792)
- Youth councils (paras 31–37): authorities should "support, resource and promote the creation of independent and youth-led" youth councils and formally recognise them. Rules should be co-created; members "elected or designated by young people and/or youth civil society through a fair and transparent procedure"; councils should be able to "follow and contribute to the decision-making processes"; and they should have "an adequate budgetary allocation, included in the regular budget" with "unrestricted and recurring funding". — [CG(2025)49-14](https://rm.coe.int/cg-2025-49-14finprov-en-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young/488028e792)
- Other new elements: physically and psychologically safe participation (para. 16); feedback to young people on results; youth assemblies, deliberative democracy and participatory budgeting (para. 38); youth spaces and youth centres (para. 39); school and university democratic structures (para. 42); member States to consider "lowering the voting age and the introduction of youth quotas on electoral lists" (para. 44; the memorandum refers to votes at 16); co-management as a principle. — [CG(2025)49-14](https://rm.coe.int/cg-2025-49-14finprov-en-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young/488028e792)
- Age: the Charter sets no age range. Para. 5 leaves it to national frameworks, and the memorandum notes that CoE/EC youth policy uses 13–30. — [CG(2025)49-14](https://rm.coe.int/cg-2025-49-14finprov-en-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young/488028e792)
- A stakeholders meeting on implementation was held in Strasbourg on 28–30 April 2026. — [CoE youth news](https://www.coe.int/web/youth/-/stakeholders-meeting-on-the-implementation-of-the-european-charter-on-the-participation-of-young-people-in-local-and-regional-life) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- The handout should cite three items together: Congress Resolution 516 (2025) and Recommendation 537 (2025) of 29 October 2025, and CM/Rec(2026)3 of 12 March 2026. The CM recommendation is the instrument addressed to governments, including Moldova, Romania and Türkiye, all of them CoE members.
- Calling it the "revised" Charter is slightly off: the CoE now calls the 2025 text the "new" European Charter. The 2003 text was itself titled the "Revised" Charter.
- Para. 4 of CM/Rec(2024)6 together with para. 48 of the new Charter gives local youth councils a direct European basis to ask to be consulted on local climate and environment decisions.

### Gaps
- I did not open the final text of CM/Rec(2026)3 to check whether the CM version differs from the Congress text or sets a review period. A search summary mentions five years; this is unverified.
- I found no CoE youth sector document specifically on follow-up to CM/Rec(2024)6, such as a toolkit or monitoring report. An ERYICA seminar in November 2025 on climate action and youth information is listed on rm.coe.int, but I did not open it. Search results also mention a 10th Conference of Ministers responsible for Youth, held in Valletta on 8–9 October 2025, which adopted a "Reference Framework on a Youth Perspective" (footnote 29 of CG(2025)49-14).

## 2. Claims to verify: national frameworks (Moldova, Türkiye, Romania)

### Takeaway
Moldova: confirmed. OMEC 2057/2025 approves a new framework regulation for Local Youth Councils (members aged 14–35, elected by a general assembly of young people, two-year mandates) and repeals the 2016 order. Its date needs care, because the header says 25.10.2025 while the signature reads 25 November 2025, and it was published 30.12.2025. Türkiye: confirmed that kent konseyleri rest on Art. 76 of Law 5393 and that youth assemblies are optional bodies under the Kent Konseyi Regulation. Romania: Law 350/2006 is the basis cited by local youth councils, but I could not confirm any specific amendment introducing local youth councils. Youth Wiki says they are optional and created by local or county council decision.

### Cited Findings
**Moldova**
- "ORDIN Nr. OMEC2057/2025 din 25.10.2025 cu privire la aprobarea Regulamentului-cadru de organizare și funcționare a Consiliului Local al Tinerilor". Published 30.12.2025 in Monitorul Oficial nr. 646-650, art. 1146, and in force on publication. The signature block reads "Nr. 2057. Chişinău, 25 noiembrie 2025", signed by Minister Dan Perciun. It is based on art. 15(b) of Law 215/2016 on youth. — [OMEC 2057/2025, mec.gov.md](https://new.mec.gov.md/sites/default/files/2026-05/152274.pdf)
- It repeals Order MTS nr. T/303 of 31.10.2016, the previous framework regulation. — [OMEC 2057/2025](https://new.mec.gov.md/sites/default/files/2026-05/152274.pdf); old order: [edu.gov.md](https://edu.gov.md/sites/default/files/ordin_nr.t303_din_31.10.2016_cu_privire_la_aprobarea_regulamentului_de_functionare_a_consiliilor_de_tineret.pdf)
- Key provisions:
  - The council is "o structură neformală, apolitică, cu rol consultativ, educativ și civic".
  - Members are aged 14–35.
  - It exists in two forms: the CLT at level I (villages, communes, towns) and the Consiliul Raional/Municipal al Tinerilor (CR/MT) at level II (raions, municipalities), made up of CLT representatives or delegates.
  - Members are elected by vote at a general assembly of young people aged 14–35 domiciled in the community. The assembly is convened by an initiative group of at least 5 members, and an electoral commission runs the vote.
  - The mandate is 2 years, renewable, and the membership is partly renewed every year.
  - The local authority "asigură resurse minime" and integrates youth participation "la toate etapele ciclului de politici publice locale, prin consultări structurate".
  - The council may submit proposals to local authorities "în toate domeniile care îi vizează pe tineri" and flag service quality.
  - Local public authorities are recommended to appoint a coordinator and to budget funds.
  - The national ministry and the National Agency for the Development of Youth Programmes and Activity provide dissemination and training.

  — [OMEC 2057/2025](https://new.mec.gov.md/sites/default/files/2026-05/152274.pdf)
- The regulation does not mention climate or the environment explicitly (keyword search of the full text). — [OMEC 2057/2025](https://new.mec.gov.md/sites/default/files/2026-05/152274.pdf)
- CNTM (National Youth Council of Moldova): not researched in this pass. No source.

**Türkiye**
- The Kent Konseyi Yönetmeliği is issued under Art. 76 of Municipal Law 5393. It was originally published in the Resmî Gazete of 8 October 2006 (no. 26313) and amended in Resmî Gazete no. 27250 of 6 June 2009. Under the 2006/2009 text (Art. 12), kent konseyleri "görev alanına giren konularda meclisler ve çalışma grupları (Kadın, Gençlik, Engelliler vb.) oluşturabilir", and working rules are set by the General Assembly. — [Bağcılar copy of the regulation](https://www.bagcilar.bel.tr/BBImages/Slider/Image/kentkonseyiyonetmeligi.pdf); [Buca copy](https://buca.bel.tr/uploads/pages/%C4%B0%C3%A7i%C5%9Fleri%20Bakanl%C4%B1%C4%9F%C4%B1%2026313%20Say%C4%B1l%C4%B1%20Kent%20Konseyi%20Y%C3%B6netmeli%C4%9Fi%20Kent%20Konseyi%20Y%C3%B6netmelik.pdf) (search snippets)
- Youth assemblies are therefore optional ("oluşturabilir"), and each city council's own directive (yönerge) sets the age range. For example, Ümraniye's Gençlik Meclisi covers ages 15–28. — [Ümraniye Gençlik Meclisi Çalışma Yönergesi](https://umraniye.bel.tr/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Genclik_Meclisi_Calisma_Yonergesi.pdf) (snippet)
- Version conflict: a Şile directive cites the Kent Konseyi Regulation "10. Maddesi" as issued by the "Çevre, Şehircilik ve İklim Değişikliği Bakanlığı" in a 2019 Resmî Gazete. That ministry only got this name in 2021, so the citation is internally inconsistent. The version currently in force and its article numbering were not verified on mevzuat.gov.tr. — [Şile document](https://sile.bel.tr/uploads/cm/ec/cmecipugm000t9fo410r6rxfw/cmecipugm000t9fo410r6rxfw.pdf) (snippet)

**Romania**
- Law 350/2006 on youth (Legea tinerilor). The latest consolidation I found in results is dated 17 January 2023. Earlier amendments: OG 39/2006 (rejected by Law 3/2007), OUG 105/2006, Law 3/2007 and OG 15/2010. — [Youthpolicy.org consolidated 2010](https://archive.youthpolicy.org/national/Romania_2006_Youth_Law_Amended_2010.pdf); [BNS copy](https://www.bns.ro/images/legea-tinerilor-nr-350-2006.pdf) (search summary)
- Local and county youth councils in Romania "are consultative bodies, not representative bodies". They are not mandatory: local or county councils create them by their own decision, and more than half of county councils have done so. They are active only in some places and rarely funded. The page cites no specific law article. It was last updated 31 March 2026. — [Youth Wiki Romania 5.3](https://national-policies.eacea.ec.europa.eu/youthwiki/chapters/romania/53-youth-representation-bodies)
- National level: the National Council for Youth was set up in 2020 as an advisory structure of the Ministry of Youth and Sports with 60 youth civil society representatives. It was regulated by ministerial order in 2022, with a new regulation in June 2024. The representative National Youth Council foreseen by a 2006 law was "never implemented". — [Youth Wiki Romania 5.3](https://national-policies.eacea.ec.europa.eu/youthwiki/chapters/romania/53-youth-representation-bodies)
- Law 350/2006 art. 28 requires county councils and the Bucharest General Council to set up a youth activities fund in their budgets. The Consiliul Tineretului din România (CTR) says leaving it out could make the budget void. — [Edupedu on CTR position](https://www.edupedu.ro/autoritatile-locale-sa-constituie-in-bugetele-proprii-un-fond-destinat-activitatilor-de-tineret-conform-legii-cere-consiliul-tineretului-din-romania-neincluderea-lui-poate-atrage-nulitatea-bugetu/) (snippet; article number taken from the search summary)
- Example of a local youth council: Bucharest Sector 2's "Consiliul Local al Tinerilor" was put to public debate in December 2025, with two-year mandates (2025–2027 and 2027–2029), and amended through a public debate in July 2026. It cites art. 3 of Law 350/2006. — [Sector 2 public consultation docs](https://public.ps2.ro/Administratie%20Publica%20Locala/Informare%20si%20consultare%20publica/2025/2025-12-24%20dezbatere%20publica%20Consiliul%20local%20al%20tinerilor/05.%20Raport%20comun.pdf) (could not be fetched; snippet only). This is also a useful example of a Law 52/2003 public debate being used for a youth council decision.

### Inferences
- The handout should give the Moldovan order as "OMEC nr. 2057 of 25 November 2025 (MO nr. 646-650, 30.12.2025)" and note the internal date inconsistency, or cite it simply as "Order 2057/2025".
- Because Chișinău is a level-II municipality, the matching structure under the new regulation is a Consiliul Municipal al Tinerilor made up of delegates from local councils and assemblies, not a single CLT.
- No source supports the claim that Law 350/2006 was "amended on local youth councils". The safer wording is that Romanian local youth councils are optional consultative bodies set up by local or county council decision, with Law 350/2006 as the general framework.

### Gaps
- CNTM (Moldova): its current status and its 2025–2026 activity on climate were not checked.
- Romania: I did not verify against legislatie.just.ro (not reachable) whether any 2023–2026 law amended Law 350/2006 regarding local youth councils. I also did not verify the current text of art. 3 or art. 28.
- Türkiye: I did not verify the current consolidated Kent Konseyi Regulation on mevzuat.gov.tr, including whether a newer regulation replaced the 2006/2009 one.

## 4. 2025–2026 European-level developments

### Takeaway
The EU President's Youth Advisory Board was set up on 25 June 2025 and met von der Leyen for a second time on 22 July 2026 to discuss the post-2027 EU Youth Strategy. The Commission ran a public consultation on the post-2027 strategy from 23 April to 16 July 2026, but I could not confirm whether the Communication has been adopted. The EESC Youth Climate and Sustainability Round Tables continue twice a year, but I found no specific 2026 edition. I did not research European Climate Pact developments.

### Cited Findings
- President's Youth Advisory Board: established 25 June 2025 as a Commission expert group of delegates from national youth councils of the EU Member States and the European Youth Forum. Its first meeting, in December 2025, covered social media and young people. At the second meeting in Brussels on 22 July 2026, more than 30 young representatives discussed priorities for the post-2027 EU Youth Strategy with von der Leyen. — [European Youth Portal](https://youth.europa.eu/news/presidents-youth-advisory-board-meets-second-time-brussels_en); [DBJR](https://www.dbjr.de/en/article/presidents-youth-advisory-board-beraet-mit-ursula-von-der-leyen-ueber-jugendpolitik-nach-2027); PYAB page in Romanian: [youth.europa.eu/strategy/pyab_ro](https://youth.europa.eu/strategy/pyab_ro) (search summary)
- Post-2027 EU Youth Strategy: the current strategy runs from 2019 to 2027. A Commission Communication on a new strategy appeared with a call for feedback dated 23 April 2026 and a deadline of 16 July 2026. It is to be accompanied by Staff Working Documents on the results of the 2019–2027 strategy and on the situation of young people. A listening and co-creation process with youth stakeholders ran in 2025–2026, including a review of the European Youth Goals. — [Europalov.no (Norwegian government EEA tracker)](https://europalov.no/politikkdokument/eu-strategi-for-unge-etter-2027/id-34779); [Council doc ST-9130-2026](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-9130-2026-INIT/en/pdf) (snippets). - EESC Youth Climate and Sustainability Round Tables: hosted twice a year by the EESC with the European Youth Forum, Generation Climate Europe and the Commission. ## 5. Public consultation on draft local decisions (consultation periods)

### Takeaway
Moldova's Law 239/2008 applies to local public authorities. Most sources give a 10-working-day minimum for submitting recommendations, but the timing of the initial announcement is worded inconsistently: within 15 working days of starting drafting, or at least 15 working days before the consultation. Romania (Law 52/2003) and Türkiye (municipal council sessions and city council general assemblies) could not be verified from primary text in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Moldova, Law 239/2008: the original text required the announcement on initiating a decision to be published "în termen de cel mult 15 zile lucrătoare" from the start of drafting. The minimum term for recommendations is "10 zile lucrătoare" from publication of the announcement (art. 12(2)). — [BRI copy of Law 239/2008](https://www.bri.gov.md/sites/default/files/document/attachments/lege239-2008_transparenta%20decizionala.pdf); [CRJM 2016 analysis](https://old.crjm.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/2016-11-23-legisl-TD-propuneri-SC.pdf); [English translation, statistica.gov.md](https://statistica.gov.md/files/files/despre/legi_hotariri/engleza/Law%20No_%20239%20of%2013-11-2008%20on%20transparency%20in%20the%20decision-making%20process.pdf) (search summaries)
- Moldova, GD 967/2016 (the implementing regulation for central authorities): information on initiating drafting must be published at least 15 working days before consultation of the draft. — [HG 967/2016 copy](https://cna.gov.md/public/files/hg_967_din_09.08.2016_transp.deciz_.pdf) (snippet). Monitorul Fiscal reports an amendment introducing "cel puţin 10 zile lucrătoare" for public consultation of drafts. — [Monitorul Fiscal](https://monitorul.fisc.md/en/editorial/reglementari_noi_cu_privire_la_transparena_in_procesul_decizional.html) (snippet)
- Moldova, national platform: drafts are published on particip.gov.md. Chișinău city hall publishes its own transparency documents. — [new.chisinau.md example](https://new.chisinau.md/ro/upload/273127/attached_files/63e218de6120a.pdf) (seen in results, not opened)

### Inferences
- Safe handout wording for Moldova: "at least 10 working days to send recommendations on a draft decision, counted from publication of the announcement (Law 239/2008); check the consolidated text on legis.md".

### Gaps
- Romania, Law 52/2003: I did not verify the deadlines against legislatie.just.ro (fetch failed). My background knowledge, which is unverified and must be checked: draft normative acts are announced at least 30 working days before adoption; recommendations can be sent for at least 10 calendar days; a public debate is mandatory if a legally constituted association or another public authority requests it in writing; council sessions are public. These Law 52/2003 points need checking. Note that OUG 57/2019 (Administrative Code) also governs local council sessions.
- Türkiye: I did not verify whether municipal council (belediye meclisi) sessions are open to the public under Law 5393, which I believe is Art. 20, or whether there is any general statutory consultation period for draft local decisions. To my knowledge Türkiye has no general law equivalent to Moldova's 239/2008 or Romania's 52/2003, but this is unverified. Kent konseyi general assemblies are typically held twice a year under the Kent Konseyi Regulation, also unverified. Also unverified: whether kent konseyi decisions must be put on the next municipal council agenda, and whether participants can attend metropolitan (büyükşehir) council sessions in Tekirdağ.
- Moldova: the current consolidated wording of Law 239/2008 after its 2023–2025 amendments was not checked (legis.md returned 403).

## 6. Recent official statistics on youth participation

### Takeaway
The latest Romania-specific Eurobarometer figures I could verify are from the European Parliament youth survey FL013EP (fieldwork 25 September to 3 October 2024, ages 16–30). Young Romanians rank climate and environment lower than the EU average as an EU priority (RO 27% vs EU 33%) and report voting slightly less (RO 35% vs EU 39%). A 2026 Flash Eurobarometer on Youth and Democracy exists (fieldwork 19 June to 1 July 2026), but I found no Romania figures from it. IPU figures for the three countries could not be confirmed beyond Moldova's 2023 figure.

### Cited Findings
- EP Flash Eurobarometer "Sondaj în rândul tinerilor" FL013EP: EU27 n=25,863, Romania n=1,054, ages 16–30, online, fieldwork 25.9–3.10.2024. Q2, top EU priorities for the next five years: rising prices and cost of living EU 40% / RO 23%; environment and climate change EU 33% / RO 27%; economic situation and jobs EU 31% / RO 41%; social protection and health EU 29% / RO 31%; education EU 27% / RO 32%. Q14, ever done: voted in local, national or European elections EU 39% / RO 35%; created or signed a petition RO 26%; volunteered for a charity or campaign EU 20% / RO 16%.
